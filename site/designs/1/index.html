Its mandate

It does

  • Prepare posts: image or video, caption, alt text and a record of where every part came from.
  • Keep each post as a folder in this site's repository, readable by anyone.
  • Publish through official routes only: Buffer, a Meta partner that posts through Instagram's own API.
  • Keep this site, and say plainly what has and has not happened.

It never

  • Never post without the owner's yes for that post, written into the post.
  • Never log in to Instagram or automate a browser.
  • Never hold or show a key or token: the posting key lives only in GitHub, for one workflow.
  • Never invent a fact, a price or a customer.

How a post goes out

Five steps, one of them human

The posting key is never in a page, a commit or a chat. It sits in GitHub, where only the posting workflow can read it.

  1. Prepare

    An agent makes the image or video and writes the caption and its sources.

  2. Folder

    The post becomes a folder on this site, so the media is public before it is posted.

  3. Yes

    The owner looks and says yes. That yes is written into the post.

  4. Workflow

    A GitHub workflow, started by hand, checks the post and sends it to Buffer.

  5. Instagram

    Buffer publishes it through Instagram's API, and the post is marked as sent so it never goes twice.

What the platform allows

Instagram's official API, in brief

From Meta's developer pages, read on 22 November 2026. Buffer works within these rules.

Who
Professional accounts, business or creator (Instagram API with Instagram Login).
Publish
Images, videos and Reels, carousels of up to 20 items, and stories (content publishing).
Limits
100 API-published posts in a moving 24 hours; 400 media containers in 24 hours (media reference).
Images
JPEG, at most 8 MB, 4:5 to 1.91:1, 320 to 1440 pixels wide.
Reels
MP4 or MOV, H.264 or HEVC, 3 seconds to 15 minutes, at most 300 MB.
Media first
Instagram fetches the media from a public address, which is why each post lives on this site first.
